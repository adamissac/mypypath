Unit 10 • Lesson 3

Formatting Numbers

Overview

Round at the end, and show the precision you actually have.

Intermediate 20–25 min
← Previous Next: A Summary Table

What You Will Learn in This Lesson

By the end of this lesson, you will be able to:

  • Round for display without rounding during the calculation.
  • Say what a number of decimal places claims.
  • Format a percentage and a large number readably.
  • Report the middle with the spread beside it.

Why This Matters

Rounding early puts error into every step that follows; rounding late and too finely claims a precision the data never had. Nine observations do not support four decimal places, and printing them anyway tells a reader the answer is more certain than it is.

Step 1: Round for the reader, not for the maths

Keep full precision through the calculation and round once, at the point where a person sees it.

1

What early rounding costs

Rounding each value before averaging is a different calculation from averaging then rounding, and on enough rows the difference is visible.

import pandas as pd

s = pd.Series([1.25, 1.25, 1.25, 1.25])
print(round(float(s.mean()), 1))
print(round(float(s.round(1).mean()), 1))
2

Formatting, which is not rounding

An f-string formats for display and leaves the value alone. That is usually what you want: the stored number stays exact and only the printed one is shortened.

import pandas as pd

value = 1234567.891
print(f"{value:,.0f}")
print(f"{value:.2f}")

share = 0.0734
print(f"{share:.1%}")

Step 2: Precision is a claim

Each decimal place you print says "I know the answer this precisely". With nine observations, you do not.

1

What the extra digits mean

72.4183 over nine rows is noise dressed as certainty. One decimal place is usually generous for a mean of a small sample.

import pandas as pd

small = pd.Series([70, 72, 75, 68, 80, 71, 73, 69, 74])
print(len(small), "observations")
print(small.mean())
print(round(float(small.mean()), 1))
2

The middle needs its spread

A mean on its own hides how much the values vary. Reporting the minimum and maximum beside it costs one line and stops a wide spread being read as a tight one.

import pandas as pd

tight = pd.Series([70.0, 71.0, 72.0])
wide = pd.Series([10.0, 71.0, 132.0])
for s in (tight, wide):
    print(f"mean {s.mean():.1f} (min {s.min():.1f}, max {s.max():.1f})")

Both have a mean of 71. Only one of them supports the sentence "they scored about 71".

Step 3: When to Use It

The number a reader sees

Round once, at the end, to the precision the data supports.

f"{mean:.1f}%"

Large and small values

Thousands separators and percentages make a figure readable at a glance.

When Not to Use It

Do not round values on the way in and then compute with them. Small rounding errors add up — round only the final answer.
